**GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** — the broadest measure of US economic output,
released quarterly in three estimates (Advance, Second, Third). The Advance print
moves markets most.

**Importance: 🟠 Tier 2.** Comprehensive but backward-looking — it confirms where
the economy *was* rather than where it's heading, so it often moves markets less
than timelier data. Still a Tier-1 event when growth/recession is the dominant
market theme.

**How it moves markets:**
- **Stronger growth:** good for corporate earnings and risk appetite, but can be
  hawkish if it implies the Fed can stay restrictive → mixed for stocks, supportive
  for USD.
- **Weaker growth / contraction:** recession fear → equities down, yields down,
  but rate-cut hopes can cushion stocks.

The release also contains the GDP price deflator, an inflation reading. Watch the
mix of consumer spending, investment, and inventories, not just the headline.
