**Consumer Confidence / Sentiment (Conference Board & UMich)** — survey-based
gauges of how households feel about the economy, jobs, and their finances.

**Importance: 🟡 Tier 2–3.** Generally a secondary mover, but the University of
Michigan survey's **inflation-expectations** component can punch above its weight
because the Fed watches it for signs that inflation psychology is becoming
unanchored.

**How it moves markets:**
- **Stronger confidence:** supports the consumer-spending outlook → mildly risk-on.
- **Weaker confidence:** signals possible pullback in spending → growth concern.
- **Rising inflation expectations:** hawkish flag for the Fed → can lift yields
  even if the headline confidence number is soft.

Sentiment data is noisy and often diverges from actual spending, so markets treat
it as a sentiment check rather than hard data.
