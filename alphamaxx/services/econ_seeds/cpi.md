**CPI (Consumer Price Index)** — the headline measure of US inflation, tracking
the price change of a basket of consumer goods and services.

**Importance: 🔴 Tier 1.** Alongside the jobs report and FOMC, CPI is one of the
two or three most market-moving releases each month. It directly shapes
expectations for Federal Reserve rate decisions.

**How it moves markets:**
- **Hotter than expected (higher inflation):** raises odds the Fed stays
  restrictive/hikes → yields up, USD up, stocks (especially long-duration tech)
  down, gold mixed.
- **Cooler than expected:** fuels rate-cut hopes → yields down, stocks up,
  USD down. "Bad news for inflation is good news for risk assets," and vice versa.
- Markets care most about the **month-over-month core** print (see Core CPI) and
  the surprise versus the forecast, not the absolute level.

Watch the reaction in the 2-year Treasury yield and Fed-funds futures for the
cleanest read on how the market is repricing rate expectations.
